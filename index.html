Find your color

Tap or drag to choose a color. The center is white; the edge is vivid. With the wheel focused, use Left/Right for hue and Up/Down for saturation. Use Brightness to adjust the light level.

Color is shared with Text and picture presets. Rhythm waves, rings, and bars use rainbow colors; Flashing Text and Pic use their selected content colors. Surplife sends hue and intensity; saturation and white are approximated by the device.

Set each pixel

Choose a 20 × 20 map file from ./map to edit its pixels.

Tap a pixel to turn it on or off. Use arrow keys to move and Space or Enter to toggle. Loaded maps keep their pixel colors. Turning on a pixel uses the selected color; changing Pixel color recolors all lit pixels. Brightness applies to all pixels. Changes send automatically when connected. Mirror display flips the preview and curtain output.

Save map asks you to select this project’s map folder once to save into ./map. Browsers without folder access download the map; move it into ./map.

Follow the rhythm

● Red● Orange● Yellow● Green● Cyan● Blue● Violet

All Rhythm modes update columns 1 to 20, with LEDs/rows 1 to 20 in each column. Sound Waves shifts a rainbow wave pattern from column 1 toward column 20, then wraps to column 1, to the music and changes rainbow colors only on detected beats. Sound Ring shows a rainbow outline with audio-driven spikes and cycling colors. Equalizer Bars shows regular rainbow bars filling from LED/row 1 toward 20 according to each bass-to-treble band's strength, with no fixed minimum height; silent bands turn off. The wave holds its position during silence, and the ring stays visible. Waves and ring add or remove up to 24 dots per update; equalizer bars follow their current audio heights directly. Flashing modes update their full pattern on detected beats, then turn off between beats. Ring and equalizer colors cycle each frame. Pixel updates never clear the whole curtain in one command. Complex frames may still take seconds to transmit; the preview animates faster than the curtain.

Start to listen. Connect a curtain to send the beat response.

Audio is analyzed locally. Microphone permission is requested only when you start. Live response needs this app in the foreground; Bluetooth updates are paced and may lag the preview.

Flip left and right across all mode previews. Matrix, Text, Pic, and all Rhythm options also send mirrored pixels to the curtain. Solid colors look unchanged. Built-in Effects mirror the preview only; the device protocol has no documented effect mirror command.

Match your curtain dimensions and wiring (up to 400 LEDs). Default addressing runs down each column, with strips ordered right to left from the top right. Text supports static, scrolling, and flashing frames; Pic paints static frames; uploaded GIFs use their first frame. Pictures use a reduced hue/intensity palette for practical BLE transfer times. Experimental painting requires compatible Surplife firmware.

Dim to zero sends a black color. The supplied notes do not specify a power-off command.

Connection & protocol settings

Close the Surplife app and any other apps connected to the curtain, then select it in the BLE picker. The controller automatically looks for compatible controls. Settings take effect on the next connection.

The original clock sample has two unexplained trailing bytes; the linked update uses a 10-byte timestamp. This controller uses the updated timestamp by default. Segmentation descriptions also differ; start with the 5-byte V0 header.

Color calibration

Test each physical channel and note its color, then select a mapping. “Save mapping” writes the selected order to the curtain’s memory.
